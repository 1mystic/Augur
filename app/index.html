How it works

From a screenshot to a lesson in three steps

01

Scan

Paste, drop or open a screenshot from chess.com, Lichess or any app. A tiny neural network finds the board and reads every square in under a second.

02

Analyse

Stockfish 19 runs in a Web Worker. See the top moves, play both sides, or let the bot take over from the position on your board.

03

Learn

Every move is graded and explained: forks, pins, hanging pieces, missed mates. Review a whole game and try your own lines at any step.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Augur is open source (GPL-3.0), runs on your own device, and needs no account. The only optional extra is your own AI API key for conversational explanations, which you pay your provider for directly.

Where do my games go?

Nowhere. Screenshots, positions and games stay in your browser or desktop app. Share links carry the moves inside the link itself, with nothing uploaded.

How strong is the engine?

It is Stockfish 19 compiled to WebAssembly, the same engine family used by Lichess and Chess.com. The bot plays at three strengths, from forgiving to full power.

How do practice lessons work?

In Tutor mode, open the Practice tab. Augur finds the moves that cost you the most, and a small model running in your browser reads what each one was about, like a fork or a weak back rank. You can also type what keeps going wrong, or pick a theme. You get five puzzles at your level, built from real games and checked by Stockfish, and a rating for each idea that adapts as you go.

Does the tutor need an API key?

No. Without one, explanations come from built-in templates that use the engine's own numbers and the tactics found on the board. Add a key in Settings if you want a more conversational tutor.

Which sites can I scan or import from?

Screenshots from chess.com, Lichess, apps and even video frames. For game review, paste a PGN from chess.com, Lichess or any other source.
